Customer journeys

one product, two ways to use it

One homepage spoke to customers who need enrichment in two different ways, and the one who wants to start alone, today, had no clear way in.

01

Context

Dyver is used in two ways.

Self-service customers run projects from a spreadsheet, on their own. Enterprise customers run enrichment inside their systems. The homepage sent nearly every call to action to a demo or to sales. I found the gap and designed the journeys.

02

Problem

The self-service customer had no primary entry point on the page.

Marketplaces are both customers at once. The marketplace's own team runs its catalog the enterprise way, with enrichment inside its systems. Its merchants are self-service customers: each one enriches its own listings, on its own, through an integration built for that marketplace. Neither side had a way in that spoke to it.

03

Process

A critique with a persona pass.

  1. Critique with a persona pass. The merchant evaluating alone needs three answers: can I do this myself, what will it cost, can I just start. The page gave none.
  2. Separated the two needs. Self-service work is a project: a file, a job of unpredictable size and timing, started today. Enterprise work never finishes: new suppliers, channels and markets keep arriving, so enrichment has to run inside the PIM, ERP and feeds, on a schedule. A marketplace is not a third kind. It needs both, one for its sellers and one for its own catalog.
  3. Decided the homepage should route, not argue. Two journey buttons, each with one line on when to choose it.
  4. Ruled out: "Start free trial" (self-service has no subscription, no clock and no card, so "trial" promises an expiry that does not exist), the reason to choose hidden behind a tap on phones (a touch device has one gesture on that button and it belongs to the link, so the line stays on the page there).
04

Proposal

A page and a funnel per way of working.

  • A page and a funnel per way of working, each written in its customer's terms and ending in the step that fits: sign up, or scope an integration.
  • A homepage that routes to them, with a third, quieter path for people who want to watch first.

Result

The self-service customer has a primary entry point.

  • The self-service customer has a primary entry point on the homepage, where every prominent button used to lead to sales.
  • Each customer reads a page about their own way of working, and reaches its action without passing through the other one's pitch.
